Testing Cybersickness in Passive and Active VR Locomotion

This project addresses a critical challenge in immersive design: how to support movement in virtual space without triggering discomfort, disorientation, or simulation sickness. Passive and active locomotion methods are both widely used in VR, yet each affects the user differently. Passive locomotion is often perceived as smoother and less physically demanding, while active movement can feel more direct and engaging. The project examines how these differences influence comfort, ease of navigation, and the user’s overall sense of immersion.

The methodology centers on comparative experimental design. Participants experience controlled locomotion conditions in virtual environments, and their performance, symptoms, and subjective responses are recorded. By evaluating comfort levels alongside motion efficiency and perceived control, the project identifies where each movement model succeeds and where it fails. This type of testing is essential because discomfort in VR is not only a usability issue; it directly affects the quality of presence and the user’s willingness to remain engaged with the system.

Beyond the comparative findings, the work contributes a broader design lesson: virtual movement cannot be designed by intuition alone. It requires a careful balance between sensory experience, system logic, and the user’s physical tolerance. In this sense, locomotion design is as much about ergonomics and perception as it is about interaction mechanics. The project demonstrates that the most effective VR systems are those that respect the body’s limits while preserving the emotional and spatial richness of the experience.

Project outcomes reinforce the importance of designing for comfort as a first-class design goal. In immersive media, movement quality shapes trust, orientation, and attention, and it can determine whether a virtual environment feels exciting or overwhelming. This project therefore contributes to a more humane and user-centered approach to VR design, where the needs of the body are treated as essential to interface quality.

Tech: Virtual reality, user testing, locomotion system design, comfort analysis, immersive prototypes.

Role: Research, UX Evaluation, VR Interaction Design, User Experience Testing.
