Testing Cybersickness in Passive and Active VR Locomotion

This project examines how different locomotion models influence cybersickness in virtual reality, with a particular focus on the relationship between movement design and bodily comfort. Passive and active locomotion are both common in immersive systems, yet they trigger different expectations in the user: one often feels smoother and less physically demanding, while the other may feel more active and direct. The research investigates how these design differences affect discomfort, orientation, and the quality of the virtual experience.

The methodology uses a comparative experimental framework grounded in benchmark testing and recorded discomfort responses. Through repeated exposures in controlled virtual environments, the study measures body sway, discomfort ratings, and observed patterns of simulation sickness to understand how the movement system changes the user’s experience. This makes the work more than a technical comparison: it is a study of how interface design intersects with human physiology and spatial perception.

The project also considers mitigation strategies, including dynamic field-of-view reduction and other comfort interventions that may reduce the impact of sensory mismatch. By identifying where discomfort emerges and which design choices increase or reduce it, the work offers actionable guidance for building more humane virtual experiences. In immersive design, comfort is not a secondary concern; it is a prerequisite for trust, attention, and sustained engagement.

Project outcomes emphasize the importance of designing movement systems around human tolerance, not just technical novelty. The findings contribute to a broader understanding of how spatial interfaces must respect bodily limits while still delivering compelling immersive experiences. This research is especially relevant for educational, design, and simulation environments where users may spend extended periods moving through virtual worlds.

Tech: Virtual reality, locomotion testing, comfort analysis, immersive prototyping, human factors research.

Role: Research, VR interaction design, evaluation, human-centered experimentation.
