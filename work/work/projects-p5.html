Testing Cybersickness in Passive and Active VR Locomotion

This project investigates how different locomotion models affect user comfort, simulation sickness, and presence in virtual reality. Passive and active movement systems each offer unique advantages: passive locomotion can reduce physical effort and maintain focus on the environment, while active locomotion often feels more engaging and intuitive. The challenge is to understand how each approach affects the user’s sensory balance and how that balance shapes immersion and usability.

Using controlled VR scenarios, the project compares movement methods under comparable conditions to assess not only performance but also discomfort and cognitive load. The study takes a user-centered perspective, measuring symptoms of cybersickness alongside behavioral responses to determine which locomotion style supports more natural and sustainable interaction in immersive environments.

The work is relevant to any interface where the body and perception must be carefully managed in digital spaces. It demonstrates that movement design is not simply a technical choice but a core factor in shaping how comfortable, legible, and persuasive a virtual experience can be.

Tech: Virtual reality, user testing, locomotion system design, comfort analysis, immersive prototypes.

Role: Research, UX Evaluation, VR Interaction Design, User Experience Testing.
