Reading a Remote Teammate’s Body

This project examines how remote collaboration can become more perceptive, humane, and emotionally intelligent when digital systems reveal subtle signals of attention, stress, and presence. In distributed teams, communication often occurs through text, video tiles, and task management tools, yet much of the body language that helps people coordinate effectively remains inaccessible. The project asks how interface design can support a richer understanding of a teammate’s state without requiring explicit self-reporting or turning collaboration into a surveillance system.

The methodology combines research in collaborative work, interaction design, and behavioral communication. I explored how people infer attention, availability, and emotional state in digital environments and translated those observations into interface structures that communicate meaning through low-friction cues. The goal was to create a system that supports awareness without overwhelming the user, balancing legibility with dignity. The design process emphasizes the difference between visible data and meaningful social interpretation: information must not only be present, but also contextually understandable and ethically framed.

In practice, the project treats collaboration as a sensory and social experience rather than a purely transactional one. It proposes that remote interfaces can become more supportive if they carry some of the subtle cues that people naturally read in shared physical rooms. This includes awareness of focus, conversational readiness, and stress levels, all translated into visual signals that help teams coordinate more thoughtfully and empathetically. The result is a design approach grounded in communication theory, user experience principles, and the emotional realities of distributed work.

Project outcomes reinforce the idea that better remote collaboration depends as much on emotional clarity as on efficiency. When teams can read one another more richly, communication becomes more responsive and humane. This project therefore contributes to a broader design agenda in which digital systems are not only tools for productivity, but also instruments for mutual understanding.

Tech: Interface prototyping, collaborative UX systems, interaction design, remote communication research.

Role: Research, UX Design, Interaction Systems, Team Collaboration Design.
