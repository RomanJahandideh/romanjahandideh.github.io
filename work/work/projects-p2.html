Reading a Remote Teammate’s Body

Reading a Remote Teammate’s Body extends the idea of bioadaptive environmental feedback into collaborative work, asking how distributed teams might better read attention, effort, and emotional state without defaulting to explicit check-ins or overloaded dashboards. The project explores ambient physiological awareness as a way to support remote collaboration in settings where shared context is easily lost. Rather than exposing raw numbers, the system translates subtle signals of stress, focus, and calm into social cues that are legible in the flow of a team interaction.

The research is grounded in the observation that collaboration depends not only on task alignment but also on relational awareness. In co-located work, people naturally notice posture, tempo, hesitation, and energy. In remote work, these signals are often fragmented or absent. This project investigates how digital systems might recover some of that contextual richness by communicating a teammate’s internal state in a restrained, considerate, and ethically framed way. The goal is not surveillance; it is better collective understanding.

The methodology combines interaction design, physiological sensing, and collaborative systems research. The project examines how users interpret ambient cues in a synchronous environment and whether those cues improve coordination, empathy, and responsiveness. The design emphasizes low-friction communication: bodily information is translated into modest, interpretable signals rather than intrusive diagnostics. The research contributes to a broader discussion about the ethics of sharing physiological information in collaborative settings, especially when that information is meant to support trust rather than control.

Project outcomes suggest that effective remote collaboration relies on more than efficiency metrics. When people can sense a teammate’s availability, burden, or stress with greater nuance, the team can respond more thoughtfully and humanely. This project therefore contributes to a design agenda in which remote interfaces support social intelligence, emotional clarity, and more relationally aware work practices across distance.

Tech: Remote collaboration interfaces, physiological awareness systems, UX prototyping, human-centered interaction design.

Role: Research, UX design, collaborative systems, interaction and communication design.
