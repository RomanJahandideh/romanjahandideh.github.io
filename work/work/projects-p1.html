Embedding Physiological Environments

Embedding Physiological Environments investigates how heart rate and heart rate variability (HRV) can be translated into a spatial and atmospheric form rather than presented as detached metrics on a dashboard. The project reimagines physiological data as part of the environment itself: as a user becomes more relaxed or more stressed, the virtual world shifts in response through changes in fog, light, vegetation, motion, and visual clarity. In this way, the body is not externalized as a graph; it becomes an active source of meaning within the experience.

The central research question asks how digital environments can communicate internal states without flattening them into clinical numerics. The project explores embodied interfaces in which the atmosphere becomes the feedback mechanism. Calm physiological states produce a clearer, more regenerative environment, while stress and arousal alter the visual field in ways that feel more unstable or saturated. This turns physiological awareness into a lived, sensory experience that can be felt and interpreted through perception rather than simply read as data.

The methodology combines physiological sensing, interaction design, and experiential prototyping. A baseline is established from Apple Watch heart-rate and HRV signals, and real-time data are normalized into regulation zones that drive changes in the environment. The system is designed to support reflection on bodily states through a responsive spatial ecosystem rather than a rigid user interface. A within-subjects evaluation with 24 participants compares the bioadaptive environment against a control condition that changes on a fixed schedule, allowing the study to assess how environmental responsiveness affects attention, affect, comfort, and immersion.

The study findings point to the value of creating environments that respond gently and meaningfully to the body. Participants showed lower average heart rate, higher HRV, and higher levels of calmness in the adaptive condition, while also reporting stronger feelings of mindful attention and flow. The work suggests that physiological feedback can become an expressive design material when it is woven into environmental behavior, supporting self-awareness and emotional regulation through indirect, embodied cues rather than explicit numerical display.

Tech: Unity, Apple Watch, heart-rate and HRV monitoring, real-time signal processing, adaptive environmental systems.

Role: Research, interaction design, system prototyping, physiological experience design.
