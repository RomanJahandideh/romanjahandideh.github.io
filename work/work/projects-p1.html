Embedding Physiological Environments

This project explores how physiological signals can be transformed from hidden data points into visible environmental behavior. Instead of representing heart rate or stress as a numeric chart, the work embeds these signals directly into the design of the space itself. As the user becomes more physiologically activated, the environment responds through changes in fog density, ambient lighting, motion, and visual clarity, turning the body into an active design input.

The goal is to investigate how interaction design can become embodied rather than purely visual. By integrating wearable sensing with environmental response, the project asks whether a digital world can become more legible through the user’s own bodily state. This creates a feedback loop in which emotional or physiological condition is not just measured but expressed spatially.

Developed as an interactive prototype, the project combines physiological signal processing, environmental simulation, and response design to produce a living, adaptive system. The result reframes the interface as a responsive atmosphere, where user awareness and bodily regulation become part of the experience of navigating and understanding the environment.

Tech: Unity, wearable biosensor integration, real-time signal processing, custom environmental state logic.

Role: Interaction Design, Research, Prototyping, Experience Design.
