Embedding Physiological Environments

This project investigates how physiological data can be transformed from an abstract diagnostic signal into a meaningful spatial experience. Rather than presenting heart-rate information as a graph or dashboard, the system embeds it into the behavior of the environment itself. As a user’s physiological state shifts, the virtual world responds through changes in visibility, atmosphere, and spatial stability, making the body an active participant in the design rather than an external observer.

The core research question concerns the relationship between embodiment and environmental design: how can digital spaces communicate internal states without reducing them to purely numerical or clinical representations? The project explores this concept through a bioadaptive interaction model in which the atmosphere becomes the interface. Fog density, lighting, motion, and environmental clarity are recalibrated in response to physiological activation, creating a direct feedback loop between body and space.

The methodology integrates experiential prototyping, signal processing, and iterative design. The project begins with a review of affective and physiological computing, followed by the development of a responsive environment in which sensed input is normalized, smoothed, and mapped to adaptive system states. This is then tested in interaction scenarios where users encounter changing environmental conditions and reflect on how those changes shape their sense of awareness, comfort, and agency. The process emphasizes that emotional and bodily states are not merely data to be measured; they are lived experiences that can be expressed through sensory and spatial design.

Project outcomes suggest that environmental responsiveness can deepen immersion by making physiological awareness feel embodied and meaningful. Instead of presenting the body as a detached source of metrics, the work makes the user’s internal condition visible through the atmosphere itself. This broadens the potential of adaptive design, especially in applications where emotional regulation, mindfulness, self-perception, and presence are central to the experience.

Tech: Unity, biosensor integration, real-time physiological data processing, adaptive environmental systems.

Role: Interaction Design, Research, System Prototyping, Experience Design.
