Optimizing Eye-Tracking Locomotion in Virtual Environments

This project explores the integration of eye-tracking into virtual locomotion as a means of creating more natural, comfortable, and context-aware movement in immersive environments. In conventional VR systems, user motion is often controlled through handheld input or controller-based movement, which can produce a mismatch between visual attention and physical action. The project asks whether gaze can function as a more intuitive control signal that supports direction, navigation, and user orientation without amplifying discomfort or interrupting presence.

The methodology follows a user-centered research approach. Controlled virtual scenarios are designed to compare eye-tracking-based movement against more familiar locomotion methods while measuring navigation efficiency, user comfort, spatial awareness, and subjective preference. The work involves iterative prototype testing and observation, allowing the design to evolve based on how participants actually experience acceleration, turning, and directional control within the environment. This process reflects a deeper design principle: movement in VR should not only feel responsive, but also support the user’s cognitive and physical comfort.

From a broader design perspective, the project contributes to the conversation around embodied interaction in immersive media. It suggests that the most effective systems are not necessarily those that maximize control complexity, but those that align interaction with natural human behavior. By considering gaze as an input modality, the project proposes a way to reduce friction between intention and action, making virtual navigation more fluid and immersive.

Project findings point toward the importance of designing interfaces that respect how users perceive and move through space. The research demonstrates that interaction design in VR is not a purely technical optimization problem; it is also an experience problem shaped by comfort, attention, and bodily perception. This makes the project especially relevant for training, simulation, and exploratory interfaces where movement quality directly shapes the quality of the experience.

Tech: Virtual reality, eye-tracking systems, immersive interaction design, usability evaluation, motion analysis.

Role: Research, VR Interaction Design, User Testing, Evaluation.
