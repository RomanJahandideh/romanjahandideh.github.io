Optimizing Eye-Tracking Locomotion in Virtual Environments

This project explores how eye-tracking can improve movement and navigation inside virtual environments, especially when comfort and spatial orientation are critical. Traditional locomotion techniques often rely on handheld controls or joystick-based movement, which can lead to discomfort, disorientation, or reduced immersion. By shifting part of this interaction to gaze-based input, the study examines whether movement can become more natural, efficient, and less disruptive in immersive experiences.

The work combines VR interface design with user evaluation to assess how eye-gaze movement affects perception, navigation accuracy, and comfort. Participants interact with controlled virtual environments while movement strategies are compared against more conventional approaches. The project focuses on measurable experience factors such as task completion, direction awareness, physical discomfort, and perceived control.

Beyond technical optimization, the project contributes to a broader conversation about spatial interaction in VR. It asks how interface systems should respond to both the user’s body and the virtual world, with the aim of creating locomotion methods that support immersion without forcing users into unnatural control schemes. The resulting design approach makes movement feel more intuitive while preserving a strong sense of presence.

Tech: Virtual reality, eye-tracking systems, immersive interaction design, usability evaluation, motion analysis.

Role: Research, VR Interaction Design, User Testing, Evaluation.
