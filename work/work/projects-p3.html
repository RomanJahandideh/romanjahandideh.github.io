Optimizing Eye-Tracking Locomotion in Virtual Environments

This project investigates eye-tracking as a locomotion input for immersive virtual environments, with a focus on comfort, spatial orientation, and natural interaction in architectural and landscape contexts. Traditional controller-based movement can interrupt attention by demanding explicit commands that are separate from visual exploration. Eye-tracking offers a different model: movement is linked to visual attention, creating a more direct correspondence between what the user looks at and how the environment responds. The research explores whether this alignment can support more intuitive and less cognitively disruptive navigation.

The methodology follows a user-centered experimental approach. A prototype system is developed to compare gaze-guided locomotion against conventional movement methods while measuring navigation performance, perceived discomfort, spatial understanding, and presence. The goal is to determine whether eye-based motion can improve users’ sense of alignment with the environment without compromising clarity or comfort. This emphasis on embodied interaction reflects a broader interest in how immersive systems can be designed to support the body’s natural tendencies rather than interrupt them.

Architectural and landscape visualization are especially relevant test cases because users must read space while moving through it. In these applications, an interface that feels comfortable and spatially legible is as important as one that is technically functional. The project therefore sits at the intersection of perceptual design, experimentation, and immersive interaction, with attention to how movement and attention shape one another in real time.

Project findings emphasize the importance of aligning input modality with perception and experience quality. While locomotion systems are often evaluated on efficiency alone, the research shows that movement quality also depends on comfort, orientation, and the user’s sense of immersion. This makes the project relevant for simulation, training, and spatial storytelling environments where navigation must feel both reliable and embodied.

Tech: Virtual reality, eye-tracking systems, immersive interaction design, spatial usability evaluation.

Role: Research, VR interaction design, user testing, evaluation.
