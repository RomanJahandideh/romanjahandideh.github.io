Creating Immersive Virtual Landscapes

This project investigates how digital landscapes can be designed to support depth perception and spatial clarity in head-mounted virtual environments. In immersive settings, users interpret distance and scale through a complex combination of color, luminance, transparency, and atmospheric cues. The project explores how carefully calibrated environmental composition can make a virtual space feel both believable and navigable, even when visual realism is intentionally limited.

The methodology draws on both design practice and perceptual research. The work begins with a study of environmental cues that influence depth judgment, followed by the construction of controlled virtual landscapes that manipulate those cues systematically. By testing combinations of value contrast, color relationships, and transparency, the project identifies which design strategies most effectively guide spatial interpretation in immersive settings. This process reflects a broader interest in understanding how visual design decisions shape human comfort, orientation, and sense of presence.

What makes the project compelling is that it treats immersive landscape design as a communicative act rather than a purely aesthetic exercise. The environment is not just a backdrop for action; it is a structure that teaches the viewer how to move through space. Through thoughtful composition, atmospheric layering, and perceptual experimentation, the project offers a framework for creating environments that are visually expressive while remaining readable, usable, and emotionally coherent.

Project outcomes suggest that good virtual landscape design is grounded in both technical control and human perception. The work contributes to a wider design discourse on how immersive systems should support orientation, reduce ambiguity, and establish a strong sense of scale. In doing so, it demonstrates that virtual landscapes can be designed not only to look compelling, but also to function as meaningful and legible spatial interfaces.

Tech: Unreal Engine, immersive environment design, perceptual testing, virtual scene construction.

Role: Research, Environment Design, VR Experience Development, User-Centered Design.
