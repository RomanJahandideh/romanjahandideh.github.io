Research projects and publications

My research examines how digital environments can become more responsive, legible, and attentive to the people who inhabit them. It brings together physiological computing, embodied interaction, virtual environment design, and spatial perception. Across these projects, I combine system development with empirical evaluation to study how people experience responsive spaces through movement, vision, and bodily signals. This collection includes published work, ongoing studies, and projects that continue to develop through peer review. Together, they reflect a research practice grounded in careful experimentation, transparent reporting, and a commitment to designing technologies that support meaningful human experience.

Embedding Physiological Environments

BioTerrarium · Unity, Apple Watch heart rate & HRV · within subjects study, n = 24

Research Objective

BioTerrarium examines how a virtual environment can communicate physiological state without relying on numerical displays. Its objective is to support awareness of bodily regulation through ambient visual feedback. The system presents a living glass ecosystem in which fog, vegetation, light, and visual clarity respond to the player's heart rate and heart rate variability. Calm physiological states support growth and visibility, while elevated stress gradually changes the atmosphere. These environmental responses make the relationship between the player's body and the virtual world perceptible without requiring attention to a conventional interface.

System Demonstration

System Implementation

Heart rate data is collected with an Apple Watch Series 7 through Apple HealthKit, transferred by a Swift application, and streamed to Unity through a WebSocket connection with less than 100 milliseconds of latency. A two minute resting baseline establishes an individual reference for each participant. Subsequent readings are normalized against this baseline and classified into four regulation zones: Coherent Calm, Focused Arousal, Stress Escalation, and Dysregulated Overdrive. Each zone influences fog density, vegetation growth, light diffusion, and shader blur at ten updates per second.

Environment States

Evaluation and Findings

I evaluated the system in a within subjects study with 24 participants. The study compared the bioadaptive environment with an identical environment that changed according to a fixed schedule. In the bioadaptive condition, participants showed a lower average heart rate, higher heart rate variability, and spent nearly 80 percent of their time in the two calmest zones. Participants also reported greater mindful attention, more positive affect, less negative affect, and deeper flow. Interview responses suggest that several participants experienced the environment as a calm and reciprocal presence.

Current Status

This work was submitted to ACM SIGGRAPH 2026 and completed a full cycle of review and revision. Although the paper was not accepted, the process produced valuable guidance for refining the study and identifying the most appropriate venue for this line of research. The system and findings continue to inform my work on physiological interaction and responsive environments.

Reading a Remote Teammate's Body

Remote collaboration · ambient physiological awareness · ACM

Research Objective

Reading a Remote Teammate's Body extends the ambient feedback principles developed in BioTerrarium from individual reflection to shared remote activity. The research examines how a collaborator's focus, strain, or calm might be communicated during screen sharing, collaborative design, and joint problem solving. Its objective is to support interpersonal awareness without requiring collaborators to interpret raw physiological measurements or divert attention from their shared task.

System Design

The project translates physiological state into subtle environmental changes within a synchronous two person experience. These cues are designed to provide a gentle sense of a collaborator's presence and condition without demanding interpretation of raw biometric data. The research asks whether this form of ambient reciprocity can restore some of the social and bodily awareness that is often absent from remote collaboration.

Current Status

The paper received a revise and resubmit decision from an ACM venue. The current revision addresses the reviewer feedback and further develops the study for a planned 2027 resubmission. The revision focuses on clarifying the interaction design contribution and strengthening the presentation of the research.

Optimizing Eye-Tracking Locomotion in Virtual Environments

Virtual Landscape Design Lab, Istanbul Technical University (dir. Dr. Ikhwan Kim) · head-mounted display research

Research Objective

This project investigates eye tracking as an input method for locomotion in virtual environments. The research addresses limitations of thumbstick and teleportation techniques, which can interrupt spatial continuity and reduce a user's sense of presence. It evaluates gaze guided locomotion as an alternative in which visual attention contributes to movement and navigation remains closely connected to the way users observe and interpret space.

Methodology

Building on my earlier research into environmental legibility, the study compares eye tracking locomotion approaches in terms of comfort, accuracy, and ease of exploration. The work is intended to support virtual architectural and landscape experiences that visitors can navigate with minimal instruction while maintaining a clear understanding of distance, direction, and spatial structure.

Current Status

This research was submitted to CAADRIA 2024, the Conference on Computer Aided Architectural Design Research in Asia. It contributes to my broader investigation of how interaction techniques and environmental cues work together to support clear and comfortable spatial experience in head mounted displays.

Creating Immersive Virtual Landscapes

Virtual Landscape Design Lab, Istanbul Technical University (dir. Dr. Ikhwan Kim) · depth perception & environmental legibility

Research Context

This research was developed during my master's studies in landscape architecture at the Virtual Landscape Design Lab at Istanbul Technical University, directed by Dr. Ikhwan Kim. The study examines how people perceive depth and distance in head mounted virtual environments. It addresses a practical challenge in architectural visualization: a landscape may appear convincing on a screen while losing depth, scale, or clarity when experienced from within a headset.

Methodology

The paper documents the design and construction of an immersive virtual landscape created to study environmental legibility. It focuses on the visual cues, spatial layers, and scale references that help viewers interpret depth and distance. These principles are particularly important when virtual environments are used to communicate unbuilt architectural and planning proposals to clients, reviewers, and communities.

Research Contribution

The findings continue to guide my approach to spatial computing and environment design. They informed later projects, including BioTerrarium, where perceptual clarity is treated as an essential part of interaction rather than a purely visual concern. The paper was published in the proceedings of eCAADe 2023.

Research study · iSpace Lab, SFU

Testing Cybersickness in Passive and Active VR Locomotion

iSpace Research Lab (Bernhard Riecke, SFU) · SteamVR, HP Reverb G2 & Meta Quest

Research Objective

This study examines how passive and active locomotion influence cybersickness in virtual reality. Its objective is to compare discomfort associated with different movement conditions and to assess the role of established mitigation techniques. Movement design remains an important barrier to comfortable and sustained VR use, particularly when visual motion does not align with movement sensed by the body. The study used the open source VR sickness benchmark developed by the iSpace Research Lab at Simon Fraser University under the direction of Bernhard Riecke.

Methodology

The benchmark records body sway before and after exposure with participants' eyes open and closed. During the virtual experience, participants report their discomfort continuously through a controller. The system can introduce mitigation strategies during a session, including static and dynamic field of view reduction, dynamic blur, and a virtual nose. It also stops movement automatically when discomfort exceeds a defined safety threshold. I used this framework to compare sickness responses during passive and active locomotion and to examine when specific mitigation strategies were most helpful.

Research Context

This project was conducted as part of the iSpace Lab's research program. My contribution focused on running the comparative study and documenting the configuration used for data collection. I maintain a fork of the benchmark on GitHub so that the study setup remains transparent and reproducible.

Course research project · IAT 813, SFU

SocialEyes: Adaptive VR Social Skills Training for Autism

Bayesian AI · gaze triggered virtual agents · Oculus Quest 3

Research Objective

SocialEyes investigates how adaptive virtual agents can support structured social skills practice for children with autism spectrum disorder. Developed as a course research project for IAT 813, the system includes sixteen agents with distinct behavioral profiles. The research objective is to create a controlled virtual social environment in which gaze initiates an agent response and the resulting feedback remains clear and manageable.

System Architecture

A Bayesian network governs each agent's response to the child's gaze and to nearby agent behavior. This probabilistic structure supports varied interactions while maintaining consistency with each agent's assigned personality. The agents communicate through six validated facial expressions: happiness, sadness, surprise, anger, fear, and neutral. Eye tracking on the Oculus Quest 3 identifies the child's focus, and only the nearest relevant agent responds so that the experience remains structured and understandable.

Technical Evaluation

The activity asks the child to identify five social friends among thirty two agents, giving each interaction a clear purpose. I evaluated the internal consistency of the artificial intelligence through a Monte Carlo simulation of thousands of gaze and gesture sequences. The simulated response rates closely matched the probabilities calculated directly from the Bayesian network, including a 24 percent simulated rate compared with a 25 percent expected rate for one gesture.

Current Status

SocialEyes is currently a proof of concept rather than a clinical intervention. Its present scope is limited to gaze based interaction within a classroom sized environment. Future development could incorporate real time facial expression recognition, allowing agents to respond not only to where a child is looking but also to the child's own expressive cues.

Independent project · interaction design prototype

A Portfolio You Walk Through: Gamifying Access to Information

Browser game · p5.js, WebGL · progressive disclosure through level design

Design Objective

This project examines spatial interaction as a method for organizing dense personal and professional information. Rather than presenting a curriculum vitae as a single linear document, the system distributes its content across a sequence of architectural spaces. Visitors progress through twelve levels, with each level revealing a new chapter after the completion of a spatial task.

Implementation

The browser experience was built with p5.js and WebGL. It translates a twelve chapter narrative, from Origins to Now, into monochrome isometric environments that visitors explore by walking rather than scrolling. A split screen mechanic presents two related states of the same space through different palettes, lighting conditions, and visible objects. This structure allows parallel information to coexist within one level and uses progression to guide attention over time.

Level Design

Design Contribution

The project serves as a personal case study in using environment as interface. Regulations, technical manuals, contracts, and other complex documents can be difficult to access when they are organized for complete linear reading. Spatial interaction offers an alternative way to encounter such material through movement, testing, and gradual disclosure. The portfolio game examines this idea at a small scale by considering how the form of access can shape the way a visitor encounters and interprets information.

Independent project · agentic AI, computational design

Parametric Massing Generator

Zoning-to-form generation · Three.js, Claude · parcel geometry through urban block scale

Research Objective

The Parametric Massing Generator investigates how zoning language can be translated into transparent and verifiable building form. Its objective is to make the relationship between written regulations, extracted constraints, and generated geometry available for inspection. The system operates at three scales: a rectangular lot, an irregular parcel, and a subdivided urban block.

System Capabilities

Users can provide a bylaw excerpt in plain language. The system extracts dimensions, setbacks, height limits, and floor space ratio while retaining the relevant source quotation and a confidence value for each constraint. It then generates the corresponding three dimensional massing envelope. A separate review pass compares the extracted constraints with the original text and proposes corrections when necessary. Conversational revisions can adjust one parameter while preserving the remaining design conditions. A built in example represents the conditional rules of Vancouver's R1 1 zoning district, including rental incentives and thresholds related to frontage and lot area.

Implementation

For convex parcels, the buildable footprint is calculated as a true inward polygon offset using a half plane intersection method. In Urban Block mode, the system subdivides a larger site and interprets directional instructions to assign residential, commercial, administrative, or park uses according to each parcel's normalized position. A three dimensional model and a color coded plan are displayed together, while every extraction, review, and revision is recorded in a visible generation log.

Access and Reproducibility

The geometric controls can be explored without an artificial intelligence key. Users who provide an Anthropic key can test source based extraction, independent review, conversational refinement, and parcel classification. The interface is designed to keep both the generated form and the reasoning behind it available for inspection.

Independent project · grounded question answering

Zoning Literacy Assistant

Regulatory literacy · retrieval, synthesis & independent verification · Claude

Research Objective

The Zoning Literacy Assistant examines how artificial intelligence can support public understanding of complex land use regulation while communicating the limits of available evidence. General purpose chat systems can provide fluent answers without sufficient support for independent evaluation. This project therefore treats source retrieval, direct citation, independent verification, and explicit uncertainty as core system requirements.

System Capabilities

A user can ask a plain language question such as, “Can I build a duplex on my lot?” The assistant answers only when the provided bylaw text contains relevant evidence, cites the exact clause it used, and states when the available material does not cover the question. A separate site assessment accepts lot dimensions and rental conditions, then reports the applicable unit tier, buildable footprint after setbacks, and estimated floor area with a citation for each result.

System Architecture

Each question moves through three distinct stages. Retrieval selects potentially relevant clauses from their titles. Synthesis develops an answer using only the selected full text. An independent verification stage checks the answer and identifies clauses that may have been missed. Site calculations are performed deterministically in code rather than by the language model. The model communicates the verified result in accessible language while preserving a traceable record of sources, calculations, and remaining uncertainty.

Access and Reproducibility

Users can test the assistant with their own Anthropic key and a set of example questions. The examples include both answerable and unsupported cases so that the system's ability to decline a question can be evaluated alongside its ability to provide a cited response. The site assessment also demonstrates how conditional floor space rules change when rental provisions are applied.

Independent project · Rhino, Grasshopper, computational geometry

Vancouver View Corridor Massing

Rhino & Grasshopper study · deterministic measurements · real City of Vancouver view corridor data

Research Objective

This project develops a computational method for generating compliant building massing from real City of Vancouver view corridor data. Protected view corridors impose an elevation limit that changes with distance from a defined viewpoint. This condition is more complex than a conventional height limit because a massing that complies with its zoning envelope may still intersect the sloped ceiling of a corridor.

Evaluation and Key Findings

The study compares precise floor by floor compliance with a conservative approach that caps an entire building at one elevation. For the selected corridor and representative site, precise geometry recovered 39.5 percent more gross floor area. A second analysis tested seven footprint rotations. Total floor area changed by less than one tenth of one percent, while the number and size of floors varied more substantially. This result follows from the linear corridor ceiling: the average permitted height over a fixed area is determined by the region's centroid and therefore remains stable when that footprint rotates around a fixed center.

Design Application

Because orientation changes floor configuration without materially changing total yield, it becomes a meaningful design variable. The project demonstrates how a language model can evaluate this verified design space in relation to an open brief, such as a preference for fewer and larger floor plates. All geometric measurements remain deterministic, while the model is used only to interpret the documented tradeoff.

Implementation and Reproducibility

The project is implemented as a Rhino and Grasshopper study using real parcel and corridor geometry. The repository includes geometry tests, a verified offline data fallback, a script that writes a structurally checked Rhino file, and a Grasshopper Python component built from the same tested core. Placeholder inputs and confirmed values are clearly identified so that the limits of each result remain visible.

Independent project · Rhino, Grasshopper, MCP, multi-agent AI

Environmental Massing Agents

Multi-agent MCP design study · real solar, carbon & wind physics · deterministic Pareto optimization

Research Objective

Environmental Massing Agents examines how specialized artificial intelligence agents can support environmental design decisions while numerical analysis remains deterministic and independently verifiable. The study defines four research questions and corresponding falsifiable hypotheses before evaluation. Each hypothesis is tested computationally, and both confirmed and unconfirmed results are reported.

System Architecture

A Generator agent proposes rotation, setback, floor height, and structural material for a massing on the Creekside Park view corridor site. Four Critic agents separately consider solar access, embodied carbon, pedestrian wind, and view corridor yield. Solar performance uses astronomical sun positions and directional shadow geometry across three representative days. Carbon uses published intensity benchmarks, wind uses a documented screening proxy, and view yield measures the portion of requested height that remains below the corridor ceiling. An Orchestrator agent reviews these perspectives and guides the next iteration through a Model Context Protocol server.

Evaluation and Key Findings

The evaluation compares NSGA II with random sampling across five seeds and an exhaustive reference grid of 1,080 designs. NSGA II did not demonstrate faster raw convergence, but it reached 95 percent of the reference threshold in four of five seeds compared with one of five for random sampling. Across 200 matched geometry comparisons, mass timber produced lower embodied carbon in every case. A convex geometry formula predicted achievable floor count within one floor across nineteen orientations. After dominant strategies were removed, wind performance and corridor yield formed a one dimensional Pareto relationship with a measured correlation of negative 1.000.

Implementation and Reproducibility

The repository provides unit tests for geometry, hypervolume, and the core NSGA II operations, together with scripts for reproducing each hypothesis test. The Model Context Protocol server can be evaluated with a scripted language model substitute or, when an Anthropic key is available, with a live model. A Grasshopper component uses the same tested geometric implementation for interactive design exploration.

Independent project · interactive 3D, serious games, evaluation design

Vancouver Climate Futures

A real-place climate serious game · grounded in cited City of Vancouver data · a real two-condition evaluation instrument

Research Objective

Vancouver Climate Futures is a serious game designed to examine whether interactive exploration influences climate risk perception and support for adaptation policy. The project draws methodological inspiration from Future Delta 2.0, a published place based climate game developed through the University of British Columbia Okanagan. It applies a comparable structure of real locations, documented evidence, consequential choices, and visible outcomes across three Vancouver contexts. The experience is implemented in Three.js so that the complete system can be evaluated and distributed through the browser.

Computational Model

Heat Vision represents a neighbourhood with limited tree canopy, Energy Vision focuses on older rental housing, and Flood Vision addresses the False Creek shoreline. Each scene presents an adaptation decision and calculates its outcome through a transparent model grounded in cited municipal and provincial evidence. The model incorporates Vancouver's 4.6 metre coastal Flood Construction Level, approximately one metre of projected sea level rise by 2100, 619 deaths during the 2021 British Columbia heat dome, a documented neighbourhood canopy temperature difference of approximately 20 degrees Celsius, and the City's Rental Apartment Retrofit Accelerator program. Player choices visibly transform the three dimensional scenes.

Iterative Design Evaluation

End to end interface testing identified two important problems in the first implementation. Large panels obscured the environment, and a disabled survey control prevented progression without explaining why. The revised design keeps the scene visible, places instructions in a compact collapsible panel, supports continuous camera control, and provides clear feedback when a survey response is incomplete. Each decision is followed by time to observe the city's response before the outcome is explained.

Evaluation Framework

The primary research question asks whether interactive causal exploration changes climate risk perception and policy support more effectively than an information equivalent static presentation. This question requires participant data, so the project includes a static control condition and a two condition evaluation instrument based on the validated New Ecological Paradigm scale. A statistical pipeline has been tested with clearly identified synthetic data containing a known effect. Separately, an exhaustive code based evaluation tested all sixteen possible choice combinations and found no violations of the documented impact model.

Access and Reproducibility

The online study includes a short initial survey, a sea level rise scenario, three adaptation decisions, and visible environmental outcomes. A companion static booklet presents the same evidence and choices without interactive simulation. The repository contains tests for every choice combination, the survey scoring logic, and the statistical workflow. These materials establish a transparent foundation for a future participant study without presenting simulated data as empirical findings.
